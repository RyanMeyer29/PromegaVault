#Definitions

A Program-Level Change Control is a master [[Definitions/Quality/Change Control\|CC]] record that provides coordinated governance for a large project involving multiple interdependent changes. It establishes overall project scope and risk assessment, and individual change controls are linked under it as actions.

A program-level CC is required when a project involves multiple interdependent changes that need coordinated oversight — for example, a multi-phase facility renovation, installation of an integrated manufacturing system spanning equipment, utilities, and facilities, or any project where individual changes cannot be fully evaluated without understanding the overall scope and cumulative risk.

The decision to initiate one is risk-based and considers project complexity, system interdependencies, and validation coordination needs. Individual CCs should not be opened until the program-level CC is in place.

## What the Parent CC Must Contain

Project scope — overall description and objectives, and identification of all affected systems, equipment, facilities, and products.

Risk assessment — evaluation of cumulative project risk (not just the risk of each individual change), plus a risk mitigation strategy if needed.

Traceability — the program-level CC lists the subordinate CCs as actions, and each subordinate CC references the program-level CC number.

## Closure

A program-level CC cannot be closed until all subordinate CCs are closed-implemented or appropriately dispositioned, and a cumulative impact assessment is complete.
