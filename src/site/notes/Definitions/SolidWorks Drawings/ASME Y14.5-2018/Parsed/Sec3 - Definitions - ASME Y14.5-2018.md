# Sec3 - Definitions - ASME Y14.5-2018

3.1 ANGULARITY — the condition of a line element, surface, feature's center plane, tangent plane, or feature's axis at an implied or specified basic angle from one or more datum planes or datum axes.

3.2 BOUNDARY, INNER (IB) — a worst-case boundary generated by the collective effects of the smallest feature of size (MMC for internal, LMC for external) and the applicable geometric tolerance.

3.3 BOUNDARY, LEAST MATERIAL (LMB) — the worst-case boundary that exists on or inside the material of a feature(s), defined by combined effects of size and geometric tolerances.

3.4 BOUNDARY, MAXIMUM MATERIAL (MMB) — the worst-case boundary that exists on or outside the material of a feature(s), defined by combined effects of size and geometric tolerances.

3.5 BOUNDARY, OUTER (OB) — a worst-case boundary generated by the collective effects of the largest feature of size (LMC for internal, MMC for external) and the applicable geometric tolerance.

3.6 CIRCULARITY (ROUNDNESS) — for a feature other than a sphere, all points of each circumferential cross-section are equidistant from the axis or spine. For a sphere, all points of the surface intersected by any plane through the center are equidistant from that center.

3.7 COAXIALITY — the condition in which the axis of the unrelated AME of one or more surfaces of revolution is coincident with a datum axis or another feature axis.

3.8 COMPLEX FEATURE — a single surface of compound curvature or a collection of features.

3.9 CONSTRAINT — a limit to one or more degrees of freedom.

3.10 CONTINUOUS FEATURE — two or more interrupted features designated with "CF" symbol, indicating they are to be considered as a single feature.

3.11 CONTINUOUS FEATURE OF SIZE — two or more regular features of size or an interrupted regular feature of size designated with "CF" symbol, considered as a single regular feature of size.

3.12 COPLANARITY — the condition of two or more surfaces having all elements in one plane.

3.13 CYLINDRICITY — the condition of a surface of revolution in which all points are equidistant from a common axis.

3.14 DATUM — a theoretically exact point, axis, line, plane, or combination thereof derived from the true geometric counterpart.

3.15 DATUM AXIS — the axis of a true geometric counterpart.

3.16 DATUM CENTER PLANE — the center plane of a true geometric counterpart.

3.17 DATUM FEATURE — a feature identified with a datum feature symbol or datum target symbol(s).

3.18 DATUM FEATURE SIMULATOR — the physical boundary used to establish a simulated datum from a specified datum feature.

3.19 DATUM REFERENCE FRAME — three mutually perpendicular datum planes and three mutually perpendicular axes at the intersections of those planes.

3.20 DATUM, SIMULATED — a point, axis, line, or plane derived from a datum feature simulator.

3.21 DATUM TARGET — designated points, lines, or areas used in establishing a datum.

3.22 DERIVED MEDIAN LINE — an imperfect line formed by the center points of all cross sections of the feature, normal to the axis of the unrelated AME.

3.23 DERIVED MEDIAN PLANE — an imperfect plane formed by the center points of all line segments bounded by the feature, normal to the center plane of the unrelated AME.

3.24 DIAMETER, AVERAGE — the average of several diametric measurements across a circular or cylindrical feature.

3.25 DIMENSION — a numerical value or mathematical expression in appropriate units used to define the shape, size, orientation, or location of a part feature.

3.26 DIMENSION, BASIC — a theoretically exact dimension. Indicated by a boxed value or noted method.

3.27 DIMENSION, DIRECTLY TOLERANCED — a dimension with an associated plus/minus tolerance or limit dimension values.

3.28 DIMENSION, REFERENCE — dimensional information usually without a tolerance, used for reference only. A repeat of a dimension or derived from other values. Does not govern production or inspection operations. Indicated by enclosing the value in parentheses. See para. 4.4.6.

3.29 ENVELOPE, ACTUAL MATING (AME) — smallest similar perfect feature contracted about an external feature or largest expanded within an internal feature at highest points. Two types: related AME (constrained to datum) and unrelated AME (not constrained).

3.30 ENVELOPE, ACTUAL MINIMUM MATERIAL — largest similar perfect feature expanded within an external feature or smallest contracted about an internal feature at lowest points.

3.31 FEATURE — a physical portion of a part (surface, pin, hole, slot) or its drawing representation.

3.32 FEATURE AXIS — the axis of the unrelated AME of a feature.

3.33 FEATURE, CENTER PLANE OF — the center plane of the unrelated AME of a feature.

3.34 FEATURE CONTROL FRAME — a rectangle containing the geometric characteristic symbol, tolerance value, modifiers, and datum references.

3.35 FEATURE OF SIZE — instances in which both regular and irregular features of size apply.
  3.35.1 Irregular Feature of Size — (a) directly toleranced feature containable by AME that is sphere, cylinder, or parallel planes; (b) directly toleranced feature containable by other AME shapes.
  3.35.2 Regular Feature of Size — one cylindrical surface, spherical surface, circular element, or set of two opposed parallel line elements/surfaces with a single directly toleranced dimension.

3.36 FEATURE-RELATING TOLERANCE ZONE FRAMEWORK (FRTZF) — the tolerance zone framework controlling basic relationship between features in a pattern, constrained in rotational DOF relative to datum features.

3.37 FLATNESS — the condition of a surface or derived median plane having all elements in one plane.

3.38 FREE STATE — the condition in which no externally introduced forces other than gravity are applied to a part.

3.39 INTERRUPTION — a gap or gaps in a feature dividing it into two or more features.

3.40 LEAST MATERIAL CONDITION (LMC) — the condition where a feature of size contains the least material within stated limits (max hole diameter or min shaft diameter).

3.41 MAXIMUM MATERIAL CONDITION (MMC) — the condition where a feature of size contains the maximum material within stated limits (min hole diameter or max shaft diameter).

3.42 NONUNIFORM TOLERANCE ZONE — an MMB and LMB where at least one boundary is a specified shape not a uniform offset from true profile.

3.43 PARALLELISM — the condition of a line element, surface, tangent plane, center plane, or axis at a basic 0° angle relative to one or more datum planes or axes.

3.44 PATTERN — two or more features to which a position or profile geometric tolerance is applied, grouped by nX, COAXIAL HOLES, ALL AROUND, ALL OVER, between/from notation, simultaneous requirements, or INDICATED.

3.45 PATTERN-LOCATING TOLERANCE ZONE FRAMEWORK (PLTZF) — the tolerance zone framework controlling basic relationship between features in a pattern, constrained in translational and rotational DOF relative to datums.

3.46 PERPENDICULARITY — the condition of a line element, surface, tangent plane, center plane, or axis at a basic 90° angle relative to one or more datum planes or axes.

3.47 PLANE, TANGENT — a plane that contacts the high point(s) of the specified surface.

3.48 POSITION — the location of one or more features of size relative to one another or to datums.

3.49 PROFILE — an outline of a surface or shape made up of one or more features, or a two-dimensional element of features.

3.50 REGARDLESS OF FEATURE SIZE (RFS) — a condition in which a geometric tolerance applies at any increment of size of the unrelated AME.

3.51 REGARDLESS OF MATERIAL BOUNDARY (RMB) — a condition in which a movable true geometric counterpart progresses from MMB toward LMB until it makes maximum contact with the extremities of a datum feature.

3.52 REPRESENTED LINE ELEMENT — a supplemental geometry line or curve segment indicating the orientation of a direction-dependent tolerance.

3.53 RESTRAINED — the condition in which externally induced forces in addition to gravity are applied to a part.

3.54 RESULTANT CONDITION — the single worst-case boundary generated by collective effects of MMC or LMC, geometric tolerance, size tolerance, and additional geometric tolerance from departure from material condition.

3.55 RUNOUT — applies to both circular and total runout.
  3.55.1 Circular Runout — each circular element of a surface is at zero variation relative to a datum axis.
  3.55.2 Total Runout — all elements of a surface or tangent plane are at zero variation relative to a datum axis.

3.56 SIMULTANEOUS REQUIREMENT — two or more geometric tolerances applying as a single pattern or part requirement.

3.57 SIZE, ACTUAL LOCAL — the actual value of any individual distance at any cross section of a feature of size.

3.58 SIZE, LIMITS OF — the specified maximum and minimum sizes.

3.59 SIZE, NOMINAL — the designation used for general identification.

3.60 STATISTICAL TOLERANCING — assigning tolerances based on statistics (assembly tolerance = square root of sum of squares of individual tolerances).

3.61 STRAIGHTNESS — the condition in which an element of a surface or derived median line is a straight line.

3.62 TOLERANCE — the total amount a dimension or feature is permitted to vary. The difference between maximum and minimum limits.

3.63 TOLERANCE, BILATERAL — variation permitted in both directions from the specified dimension or true profile.
  3.63.1 Equal Bilateral — variation permitted equally in both directions.
  3.63.2 Unequal Bilateral — unequal variation permitted in both directions.

3.64 TOLERANCE, GEOMETRIC — a tolerance indicated using a geometric characteristic symbol.

3.65 TOLERANCE, UNILATERAL — variation permitted in one direction only from the specified dimension or true profile.

3.66 TRUE GEOMETRIC COUNTERPART — the theoretically perfect boundary used to establish a datum from a specified datum feature. Only applicable to datums.

3.67 TRUE POSITION — the theoretically exact location of a feature of size, established by basic dimensions.

3.68 TRUE PROFILE — the profile defined by basic radii, basic angular dimensions, basic coordinate dimensions, basic size dimensions, undimensioned drawings, formulas, or mathematical data.

3.69 UNIFORM TOLERANCE ZONE — a constant distance between two boundaries equally or unequally disposed about the true profile or entirely on one side.

3.70 VIRTUAL CONDITION (VC) — a constant boundary generated by collective effects of a feature of size's specified MMC or LMC and the geometric tolerance for that material condition.
