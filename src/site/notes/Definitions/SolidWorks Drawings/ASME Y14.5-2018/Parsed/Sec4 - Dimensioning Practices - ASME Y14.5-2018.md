## 4.1 Fundamental Rules

Dimensioning and tolerancing shall clearly define engineering intent and shall conform to the following:
(a) Each feature shall be toleranced. Dimensions specifically identified as reference, maximum, minimum, or stock do not require a tolerance.
(b) Dimensioning and tolerancing shall be complete. Neither scaling nor assumption of a distance or size is permitted.
(c) Each necessary dimension of an end product shall be shown. No more dimensions than necessary for complete definition shall be given. Reference dimensions should be minimized.
(d) Dimensions shall be selected and arranged to suit the function and mating relationship and shall not be subject to more than one interpretation.
(e) The drawing should define a part without specifying manufacturing methods.
(f) Nonmandatory processing dimensions shall be identified by a note such as NONMANDATORY (MFG DATA).
(g) Dimensions should be arranged for optimum readability.
(h) Dimensions in orthographic views should be shown in true profile views and refer to visible outlines.
(j) An implied 90° angle shall apply where center lines and lines depicting features are shown at right angles and no angle is specified.
(k) An implied 90° basic angle shall apply where center lines of features shown at right angles are located by basic dimensions and no angle is specified.
(l) A zero basic dimension shall apply where axes, center planes, or surfaces are shown coincident and geometric tolerances establish the relationship.
(m) UOS, all dimensions and tolerances are applicable at 20°C (68°F).
(n) UOS, all dimensions and tolerances apply in a free state condition.
(o) UOS, all tolerances and datum features apply for full depth, length, and width of the feature.
(p) Dimensions and tolerances apply only at the drawing level where they are specified.
(q) UOS, the as-designed dimension value does not establish a functional or manufacturing target.
(r) Where a coordinate system is shown, it shall be right-handed UOS. Each axis shall be labeled with the positive direction shown.
(s) UOS, elements of a surface include surface texture and flaws. All elements of a surface shall be within applicable specified tolerance zone boundaries.

## 4.2 Units of Measure

Decimal dimensioning shall be used. SI linear unit: millimeter. U.S. Customary: decimal inch.

4.2.3 — Where all dimensions are in mm or all in inches, individual identification is not required. Drawing shall contain a note: UNLESS OTHERWISE SPECIFIED, ALL DIMENSIONS ARE IN MILLIMETERS (or IN INCHES).

4.2.5 — Angular dimensions are expressed in degrees; degrees and decimal parts; or degrees, minutes, and seconds (°, ′, ″).

## 4.3 Types of Dimensioning

4.3.1 Millimeter Dimensioning:
- Dimension less than 1 mm: zero precedes the decimal point (0.5, not .5)
- Whole number: no decimal point or trailing zero (12, not 12.0)
- Dimension exceeding whole number: no trailing zero (12.5, not 12.50)

4.3.2 Decimal Inch Dimensioning:
- No zero before decimal for values less than 1 (.5, not 0.5)
- Express dimension to same number of decimal places as its tolerance

## 4.4 Application of Dimensions

4.4.1 Dimension Lines — show direction and extent of a dimension. The following shall NOT be used as dimension lines: center lines, extension lines, phantom lines, or lines part of the object outline.

4.4.2 Extension Lines — start with a short visible gap from the part outline and extend beyond the outermost related dimension line. Extension lines are drawn perpendicular to dimension lines. Extension lines are not to be broken when crossing other lines (except permissible break when crossing arrowheads close to arrowheads).

4.4.4 Leaders — used to direct a dimension, note, or symbol. Terminates in arrowhead for edges/features. Terminates in dot when referring to a surface by ending within the outline. Should be inclined straight line with short horizontal portion extending to midheight of the first or last character.

4.4.5 Reading Direction — notes and dimensions should be placed to read from the bottom of the drawing.

4.4.6 Reference Dimensions — enclosed in parentheses. See Figures 4-17 and 4-18.

4.4.7 Overall Dimensions — when an overall dimension is specified, one intermediate dimension is omitted or identified as a reference dimension.

## 4.5 Dimensioning Features

4.5.1 Diameters — the diameter symbol (⌀) shall precede all diameter values.

4.5.2 Radii — each radius value shall be preceded by R. A radius dimension line uses one arrowhead at the arc end only, never at the radius center.
- 4.5.2.3 True Radius — where a radius is dimensioned in a view that does not show true shape, "TRUE" is added before or after the radius dimension.
- 4.5.2.4 Multiple Radii — when a part has a number of radii of the same dimension, a note may be used instead of dimensioning each separately.
- 4.5.2.5 Spherical Radii — preceded by SR.

4.5.10 Round Holes — if not clear that hole goes through, abbreviation "THRU" follows the dimension. Blind hole depth is the depth of the full diameter from the outer surface.

4.5.16 Chamfers — dimensioned by a linear dimension and angle, or by two linear dimensions.
- 4.5.16.1 45° chamfers on perpendicular surfaces may be specified by note (e.g., .XX X 45°). This method is only for 45° chamfers.
- 4.5.16.3 For surfaces intersecting at other than right angles, two acceptable methods exist — see Figure 4-43 (PDF p.29).

4.5.19 Rod and Tubing Details — defined by straight lengths, bend radii, angles of bend, and angles of twist.

## 4.6 Location of Features

4.6.5 Repetitive Features — specified by numeral followed by "X" and the size dimension. No space between number and X. Space between X and dimension. Example: 4X Ø.136

4.6.5.2 Spacing — equal spacing of features: number of spaces followed by "X" and the applicable spacing dimension. Example: 3X 1.00
