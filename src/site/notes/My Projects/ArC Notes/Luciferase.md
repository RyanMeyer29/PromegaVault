#Definitions

Luciferase is an enzyme that catalyzes a light-producing chemical reaction — the same reaction responsible for a firefly's glow. It works by oxidizing a substrate called luciferin in the presence of oxygen and ATP, releasing energy as light rather than heat. The name comes from the Latin "lucifer," meaning light-bearer. Because the amount of light produced is directly proportional to the amount of luciferase present, it is extremely useful in research as a reporter — you can use it to detect and measure biological activity with very high sensitivity.

At Promega, luciferase is one of the major products produced via [[My Projects/ArC Notes/Fermentation\|Fermentation]] and feeds into a large number of Promega products. The fermentation batch that produces it is coded F884; after protein purification downstream, it becomes product E140.
